eBay titles · what the 80 are for

How many characters is an eBay title, and do they all count?

Eighty, and yes — all of them. Spaces count. Hyphens count. Exclamation marks count. There is no free punctuation. But the limit is rarely what is holding a title back; what the eighty get spent on is.

The short answer. You get 80 characters and every one is counted the same way. Most sellers use about half, then fill the rest with words buyers never type. The auditor on this site checks 13 such phrases, 12 characters that do not belong in a title at all, and a list of seller shorthand — and it is free.

A real title, measured

Vintage Pyrex Mixing Bowl Set Milk Glass L@@K NR Nice!!

55 of 80 characters used. The struck-through part is 15 of them — L@@K is filler, NR is seller shorthand for a selling format rather than a thing anyone searches for, and Nice!! describes a feeling. That is 15 characters that could have carried the pattern name, the bowl size, or the year.

The bits most advice gets wrong

  • "Use all 80" is not the advice. Using all eighty on the wrong words is worse than using fifty on the right ones. Fill them with attributes a buyer would type: brand, model, size, material, year, colour.
  • Capitals win nothing. eBay search ignores case. A title in capitals matches exactly the same searches and reads as shouting.
  • Repeating a word does not rank it twice. The second copy is spent characters that could have been a different keyword.
  • An ellipsis usually means the title was cut off. It is the clearest signal that a title was pasted from somewhere narrower, and it tells a buyer nothing.
  • Punctuation is not free. Three exclamation marks cost the same as the word "red".

Questions people actually ask

How many characters does an eBay title get?

Eighty. The free auditor on this site uses 80 as its limit because that is what eBay's listing form enforces.

Do spaces and punctuation count?

Yes. Every character counts, including spaces, hyphens and exclamation marks. There is no free punctuation — an exclamation mark costs exactly as much as a letter of your brand name.

Does writing in capitals help?

No. eBay search ignores case, so capitals match nothing extra. They cost the same as lower case and read as shouting. The auditor flags a title once 40% or more of its significant words are capitalised.

What is the single most wasteful thing people put in titles?

Subjective filler — words describing how the seller feels about the item rather than what it is. 'BEAUTIFUL' alone spends 9 of the 80 characters, and buyers do not search for it. The auditor checks 13 such phrases; together they account for 88 characters.

Is seller shorthand like 'Vtg' a problem?

It is, because it is not what buyers type. Somebody looking for a vintage bowl searches “vintage”, not “Vtg”. The three characters you save cost you the match. The auditor expands shorthand automatically when the full word still fits, and tells you to free up room first when it does not.

Where this comes from, and what it is not

Every phrase and rule on this page is read out of the free auditor's own source rather than retyped, so the page and the tool cannot drift apart. What this is not: a ranking guide. It can tell you which characters are being wasted; it cannot tell you what eBay's search will do with the ones you keep, and anyone claiming otherwise about a search engine they do not operate is guessing. Category limits can also differ, and the character counter on eBay's own listing form is the authority, not this page.
