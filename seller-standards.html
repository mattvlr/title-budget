How many late shipments before you lose Top Rated?

eBay's Top Rated late shipment limit is 5 late shipments or 3% of transactions, whichever is larger. Almost every guide quotes only the 3%, which tells a 40-order seller they can afford one late package when they can actually afford five. Put your own counts in and see the real headroom.

That the fee base is the whole amount the buyer paid is the same rule the fee comparison page is built on. The bits most guides get wrong

Five late shipments is inside the limit until you pass 199 sales

The requirement is written with two clauses and you only need to satisfy the more generous one. The fixed count of 5 is the better allowance for every seller up to 199 transactions in the evaluation period; 3% does not overtake it until 200, where it allows 6. So a seller with 40 transactions who has been told “keep it under 3%” believes one late package is the edge of a cliff. It is not — they have five. The unresolved-case requirement is written the same way, and its fixed allowance of 2 holds all the way to 999 transactions, because 0.3% does not reach 3 until 1,000.

“No more than 5 (or 3% of transactions)” — late shipment rate, Top Rated. “No more than 2 (or 0.3% of transactions)” — cases closed without seller resolution.

A package arriving late is not automatically a late shipment

eBay's stated test has two conditions joined by and, not or. A delivery scan after the latest estimated delivery date counts against you only if there was also no carrier scan within your handling time. Getting the parcel scanned by the carrier inside your handling time is the part you control, and it is what the rule actually turns on. A buyer confirming late arrival is counted separately, and that one you cannot scan your way out of.

“With tracking, we'll count an item as late when: The delivery scan is after the latest estimated delivery date AND There is no carrier scan within your handling time.” And, separately: “If the buyer confirms the item arrived after the estimated delivery date, it will be counted as late.”

The defect rate has no fixed count allowance, and that is brutal at low volume

Unlike late shipments and unresolved cases, the Top Rated defect requirement is a bare percentage. 0.5% of 100 transactions is half a defect, so one defect puts a 100-transaction seller out of Top Rated outright. You need 200 transactions in the evaluation period before a single defect fits under the line, and 400 before two do. eBay puts a second condition on the same requirement — the defects must involve no more than 3 different buyers — so both have to hold.

“No more than 0.5%, associated with no more than 3 different buyers” — transaction defect rate, Top Rated. Above Standard is “No more than 2% of transactions”.

Which window you are judged on flips at 400 transactions

More than 400 transactions in the last 3 months and you are judged on those 3 months. Fewer, and you are judged on the last 12. The same three defects are 0.75% of a 400-transaction quarter and 0.25% of a 1,200-transaction year, so a seller whose volume drifts across 400 can watch their measured rate move without anything about their selling changing. The Top Rated volume minimums — 100 transactions and $1,000 in US sales — are read over 12 months whichever window applies to the metrics.

Evaluations happen on the 20th of each month. Your level is fixed between evaluations. A defect on the 21st is measured at the next one, and a metric you repair on the 21st does not restore your level until then either.

Questions people actually ask

How many late shipments can I have before I lose Top Rated Seller?

Five, or 3% of your transactions in the evaluation period — whichever is the larger number. Up to 199 transactions the fixed five is the better clause. At 200 the percentage overtakes it and allows six, and from there it grows with your volume. The calculator above works it out for your own numbers.

Does a package that arrives late always count against me?

No. Per eBay's own wording it counts as late only when the delivery scan is after the latest estimated delivery date and there is no carrier scan within your handling time. Both. A buyer confirming late arrival is counted separately.

Am I judged on 3 months or 12?

More than 400 transactions in the last 3 months and it is those 3 months. Fewer and it is the last 12. The volume minimums for Top Rated — 100 transactions and $1,000 in US sales — are read over 12 months either way.

How many defects can a 100-transaction seller have and stay Top Rated?

None. 0.5% of 100 is half a defect, and there is no fixed count allowance on the defect requirement the way there is on late shipments. 200 transactions is where the first defect starts to fit, and 400 where the second does.

What is Top Rated actually worth?

A 10% discount on your final value fees, which eBay calculates on the total the buyer paid including shipping and tax. On a $29.95 total at 13.6%, the fee is $4.07 and the discount is $0.41. Worth having, not worth panicking over — and the calculator above puts your own number on it.

Can I lose Top Rated without being Below Standard?

Yes, and it is the usual case. Top Rated needs 0.5% defects; Above Standard needs 2%. Between those you are an ordinary seller in good standing who does not get the fee discount. Below Standard is a different matter — eBay states your items “may be placed lower in Best Match search results”, your “selling limits may decrease”, and you “may be charged higher final value fees”.
