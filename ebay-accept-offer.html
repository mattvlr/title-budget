eBay Best Offer · the arithmetic

Is it worth relisting, or should I take the eBay offer?

Take it, unless a full-price sale is close to certain. The reason is arithmetic almost nobody does: a discount is partly paid by the fee you no longer owe, so it costs less than its face value — and the money you spent buying the item does not belong in the decision at all.

The short answer. On a $42 listing with $8 postage, a $35 offer leaves you $29.75 in hand today against $35.80 if the full-price sale eventually happens. Holding out is the better bet only if the chance of that sale is above 83%. Ask yourself honestly how likely that is; everything below is the arithmetic behind it.

The $7 you came down cost you $6.05, not $7. eBay's fee fell by $0.95 when the price did, so the marketplace paid that much of your discount for you — 86% of the face value reaches your pocket as a loss, and the rest never existed. The item cost sits outside this table on purpose: it is spent either way, and the section below explains why putting it in here leads people to the wrong answer.

Note that the floor is lowest exactly where a discount costs most: a low-fee marketplace lets you go further down before you lose money, while charging you more for each step down.

The bits most advice gets wrong

  • What you paid for it is not part of the decision. That money is spent whichever button you press. Counting it moves the threshold on this order from 83% to 75% — the difference between taking the offer and refusing it, decided by a number that nothing you do now can change.
  • A $7 discount is not a $7 loss. It is $6.05, because the fee comes down with the price. Every comparison that treats a discount at face value overstates what you are giving up.
  • Free shipping changes the percentage, not the money. The same $7 is 16.7% off a $42 item and 14% off the same listing at $50 with free postage. eBay's fee is identical either way, because it is charged on the total the buyer paid. Only the discount looks smaller.
  • Relisting is free, and that is the trap. Six of the seven marketplaces here charge nothing to list; only Etsy does, at $0.20. Because it is free it feels like no decision — so the item goes back up unchanged and buys another stretch of silence.
  • Holding is not free, even when relisting is. The $12 in the item is money you cannot spend on the next one, and the shelf space is real. This page will not put a number on your time, because anyone who does is making it up.

Questions people actually ask

Should I accept a Best Offer or hold out for the asking price?

Hold out only if you think the chance of selling at the asking price is better than 83%. On a $42 item with $8 shipping, a $35 offer puts $29.75 in your pocket now against $35.80 if the full-price sale happens, so the offer is worth taking unless a full-price buyer is close to certain.

How much does accepting a lower offer actually cost me?

Less than the discount, because the fee is charged on what the buyer pays and a smaller payment carries a smaller fee. A $7 reduction on a $42 eBay item costs you $6.05, not $7 — eBay stops charging $0.95 of it. Every dollar you come down costs you $0.864.

What is the lowest offer worth accepting?

The one that still covers what you paid for the item, the label and the packaging. On the worked order here — $12.00 of item cost, $6.40 of postage and $0.60 of packaging — that floor is $14.46 on eBay, which is $27.54 below the asking price. Below it you are paying a stranger to take your stock.

Does what I paid for the item change whether I should accept?

No, and this is the mistake that keeps items on the shelf. The money you spent is spent whichever button you press, so it does not belong in the comparison. Counting it makes holding out look better than it is: on this order it moves the threshold from 83% down to 75%, which is the difference between taking the offer and refusing it.

Does relisting cost anything?

On 6 of the 7 marketplaces modelled here, nothing — relisting is free and the only cost of holding is the money sitting in the item. Etsy is the exception at $0.20 per listing. What relisting costs everywhere is the chance to change something: if the title is the reason nobody bid, relisting it unchanged buys another stretch of silence.

Is a discount worth more on a cheap marketplace or an expensive one?

The opposite of what people expect. The higher the fee rate, the more of your discount the marketplace absorbs, so the same $7 off costs you $5.60 on Poshmark and $6.77 on Depop. The low-fee marketplace is still the better place to sell — it just gives you less help when you negotiate.

Where these numbers come from, and what they are not

Every figure on this page is computed from the fee model inside the free fee comparison, parsed out of that page rather than retyped, so the two cannot drift apart. The rates are standard selling fees checked September 2026 and do not include promoted-listing ad rates, store subscriptions, international fees or category exceptions — eBay's rate varies by category and 13.6% is the common one, not the only one. The worked order is the same $42 item with $8 postage used on the payout page, so the two agree.

What this page does not cover

It does not tell you whether your item will sell. The last column of the ladder is a threshold, not a forecast: it says what you would have to believe to justify refusing, and the believing is yours. It does not price your time, model seasonality, or know what the item sold for last month. It says nothing about what any search engine or any marketplace's own recommendation system will do with a relisted item, because that is not knowable from here. It does not cover counter-offers, auction reserves, the offers eBay sends on your behalf to people watching, or whether a low offer deserves a reply. One thing only: the arithmetic of accepting a number, and the odds that would justify turning it down.
