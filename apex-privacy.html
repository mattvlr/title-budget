Apex Drive Log — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 September 2026

The short version. Apex records your drives into a database file on your iPhone and leaves them there. There is no account, no server of ours, and no analytics. One optional feature — reading a fuel receipt from a photo — sends that photo to OpenAI, and only if you have set up your own OpenAI API key. Nothing else you do in Apex leaves your device except the map and place-name lookups Apple's own maps need in order to draw a map.

Who is responsible

Apex Drive Log is published by an independent developer. Questions about this policy or about your data: bookmonger990@gmail.com.

What Apex records, and where it goes

Location

While a drive is recording — that is, between the moment you tap Start and the moment you tap Stop, and at no other time — Apex reads your precise GPS position roughly once a second. Each reading includes latitude, longitude, altitude, speed, heading and the accuracy iOS reports for those values.

Those readings are written to a database file inside the app's own storage on your iPhone. They are not uploaded anywhere. Apex has no server, no account system and no cloud backup. If you delete the app, the drives are deleted with it.

Apex asks for the Always location permission so that a drive you have started keeps recording when your screen locks or you switch to another app. Without it, locking your phone mid-drive ends the recording. You can decline; drives will then record only while Apex is open on screen, and the app will say so. While Apex is recording, iOS shows the blue location indicator in your status bar, so you can always tell.

Camera and photo library

If you log a fuel fillup by photographing the pump display or the receipt, that photo is stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone and shown alongside the fillup. Apex does not write anything to your photo library.

Fuel and vehicle records

Prices, gallons, totals, odometer readings and the notes on a fillup are stored on your iPhone only.

Your OpenAI API key

If you choose to use the photo-reading feature, the key you paste is stored in this app's preferences on your iPhone. It is sent only to OpenAI, as the authorisation header of the requests described below, and to nobody else. Be aware that it is stored in the app's preference file rather than in the iOS Keychain.

Data sent off your device

What Apex does not do

  • No tracking. Apex does not read the advertising identifier, does not build a profile of you, and does not share anything with data brokers or advertisers. There is no App Tracking Transparency prompt because there is nothing to ask permission for.
  • No analytics and no crash reporting. There is no Firebase, Google Analytics, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Sentry or Crashlytics in the app. Apex has no idea how you use it.
  • No advertising. No ads, no ad networks.
  • No account. Nothing to sign up for, so no email address, name or password is collected.
  • No selling of data. Apex collects almost nothing and sells none of it.

Children

Apex is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Your control over your data

  • Turn recording off: Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Apex. Apex cannot record without it.
  • Delete one drive: swipe it in the Feed.
  • Delete everything: delete the app. Its database, its fillup photos and your stored API key go with it.
  • Take your data with you: any drive can be exported as a GPX file from its detail screen and shared or saved wherever you like.

Because Apex holds nothing on a server, there is no account for us to look up, export or erase on request — everything is already in your hands, on your device.

Changes to this policy

If Apex ever starts collecting something it does not collect today, this page will be updated before the version that does it ships, and the date at the top will change.
